# Step-by-Step Instructor Script — Mission 4: Technical Communication

**Duration:** 120–140 minutes  
**Student role:** Technical Communicator  
**Instructor goal:** Teach the student to turn incomplete evidence into a six-slide decision narrative without hiding uncertainty or inventing facts.

## Essential definitions

- **Audience:** The people who must understand or act on the presentation.
- **Decision statement:** A precise description of what the audience is being asked to decide.
- **Message headline:** A slide title that states the slide’s conclusion, not merely its topic.
- **Story spine:** The sequence of claims that leads the audience from context to decision.
- **Traceability:** The ability to connect a slide claim to its source.
- **Speaker notes:** Supporting explanation for the presenter; they must not hide decision-critical uncertainty.
- **Critique:** A structured assessment of clarity, evidence, audience fit, and decision usefulness.

## Preparation

1. Open the brief, source notes, storyboard template, critique checklist, answer key, and rubric.
2. Prepare slide software and a blank six-slide deck.
3. Do not prepare a finished example deck; the student should build the argument.
4. Confirm the required deliverables: storyboard, six-slide deck, speaker notes, completed critique checklist, and one revised slide.

## Opening — 0 to 10 minutes

Say:

> “A presentation is not a report made shorter. It is a controlled path that helps a specific audience make a specific decision.”

Ask: “What should leadership be able to decide after this presentation?” Require a one-sentence answer before continuing.

## Classify the source notes — 10 to 28 minutes

Have the student label each important note as:

- fact;
- stakeholder concern;
- assumption;
- recommendation;
- unresolved question.

Ask:

- “Which statements are measured?”
- “Which are opinions or concerns?”
- “Which departments disagree?”
- “Which uncertainty must be visible to leadership?”

Do not allow disagreement to be averaged into a false consensus.

## Build the story spine — 28 to 45 minutes

The student writes six message headlines before opening slide software. Read them in order. They should form a coherent argument.

A common structure is:

1. decision and context;
2. evidence supporting the opportunity;
3. principal limitation or risk;
4. options and trade-offs;
5. bounded recommendation;
6. next decision or validation gate.

Reject topic-only headings such as “Costs.” Ask: “What is the conclusion about costs?”

## Complete the storyboard — 45 to 68 minutes

For every slide require:

- purpose;
- message headline;
- evidence and source;
- proposed visual;
- uncertainty or scope note;
- speaker point;
- audience action.

Ask the student to justify every number. For each proposed visual ask: “What becomes easier to understand because of this visual?”

## Build the rough deck — 68 to 95 minutes

1. Create all six slides using the approved storyboard.
2. Add message headlines first.
3. Add only evidence needed to support each headline.
4. Label assumptions and exclusions visibly.
5. Add speaker notes to every slide.
6. Use consistent names, dates, and terminology.

Instructor rule: do not spend lesson time choosing decorative colors or animations until the evidence and narrative are sound.

## Rehearse — 95 to 108 minutes

Have the student present within the required five-minute limit. Stop at exactly five minutes if necessary and identify where the speaking plan overran. Then ask realistic questions:

- “What source supports that?”
- “What is excluded from this figure?”
- “What happens if the pilot fails?”
- “Which uncertainty is most likely to change the recommendation?”

Require direct answers. “I do not know from the current evidence” is acceptable when followed by a validation step.

## Critique and revise — 108 to 128 minutes

The student completes the critique checklist on their own deck. Then they select one slide with the greatest decision risk and revise it.

Require a short revision note:

- weakness found;
- change made;
- why it improves the decision conversation;
- evidence that remains uncertain.

The critique must refer to the actual created deck; there is no supplied draft to critique instead.

## Debrief — final 10 minutes

Ask:

1. “Which slide carries the most consequential claim?”
2. “Which slide would change first if one source were corrected?”
3. “What did you remove because it did not affect the decision?”
4. “What uncertainty did you deliberately keep visible?”

## Minimum acceptable submission

- completed storyboard;
- complete six-slide deck;
- speaker notes for all slides;
- completed critique checklist;
- one revised slide and revision explanation;
- traceable claims and visible uncertainty;
- a rehearsal completed within five minutes.

## Intervention guide

- **Student turns every note into a slide:** Return to the decision statement.
- **Student uses topic headings:** Rewrite them as conclusions.
- **Student invents cost exclusions:** Require exact source scope.
- **Student hides caveats in notes:** Move decision-critical caveats onto the slide.
- **Student focuses on aesthetics:** Pause styling and review evidence hierarchy.
- **Student overruns five minutes:** Remove repetition, shorten transitions, and preserve the decision-critical evidence.

## Exit check

The student must define message headline, story spine, and traceability, then point to one example of each in the finished deck.
