# Instructor Guide — Mission 2: Deep Research and Information Synthesis

**Recommended duration:** 120 minutes  
**Student role:** AI Research Analyst  
**Primary outcome:** The student can compare unequal sources, preserve uncertainty, and produce a bounded recommendation without double-counting repeated claims.

## Before the lesson

Open the decision brief, all four source documents, evidence matrix, memo template, answer key, and rubric. Read the source pack once and note which claims are independent, repeated, non-comparable, incomplete, or commercially motivated.

Do not tell the student which structural option is “best.” The exercise is designed so a definitive universal winner is not supported.

## Teaching objectives

The student should learn to:

1. separate source type from source quality;
2. distinguish independent evidence from repeated evidence;
3. identify incompatible test conditions and cost scopes;
4. make recommendations that are proportional to the evidence;
5. state what research or testing should happen next.

## Lesson plan

### 0–12 min — Introduce the decision

Explain that research is not collecting the largest number of sources. It is building a defensible chain from question to evidence to recommendation.

Ask: “What could make four sources behave like only two sources?” Use the answers to introduce repeated claims and shared provenance.

### 12–30 min — Source triage

Have the student scan all sources and classify each by author, purpose, evidence type, likely incentives, and direct relevance. Ask them to predict which source is strongest for cost, dynamic behavior, manufacturability, and field risk.

Checkpoint: the student must not equate a polished document with a high-quality source.

### 30–55 min — Build the evidence matrix

For each meaningful claim, require:

- the exact source;
- the measurement or statement;
- test conditions or scope;
- limitations;
- whether another source is independent or derivative;
- decision relevance.

Pause when the distributor repeats a manufacturer claim. Ask whether the second document increases confidence and why or why not.

### 55–72 min — Compare unlike evidence

Lead a short demonstration using one pair of non-comparable measurements. Ask the student to identify the variables that prevent a direct numerical ranking.

Teaching point: “Not comparable” does not mean “useless.” It means the student must describe what each result supports and what it does not support.

### 72–95 min — Draft the research memo

Guide the student through recommendation, supporting evidence, risks, uncertainty, and next steps. Require a bounded decision such as a pilot, targeted test, or conditional selection rather than false certainty.

Use the question: “What decision can management safely make today, and what decision must wait?”

### 95–110 min — Challenge the recommendation

Play a skeptical reviewer. Ask:

- Which cost figures include different items?
- Which claim has been counted twice?
- What evidence most strongly contradicts the recommendation?
- What result would cause you to reverse the recommendation?

The student should revise the memo where necessary.

### 110–120 min — Debrief and exit ticket

Ask the student to explain the difference between source count, evidence count, and independent evidence count.

## Likely difficulties and interventions

**Difficulty:** Averaging incompatible values.  
**Intervention:** Require a test-condition column before any numerical comparison.

**Difficulty:** Treating a distributor summary as independent confirmation.  
**Intervention:** Trace the claim back to its origin and label derivative evidence.

**Difficulty:** Forcing a binary answer.  
**Intervention:** Ask what smaller, reversible decision the evidence supports.

**Difficulty:** Listing caveats without changing the recommendation.  
**Intervention:** Require every major limitation to affect confidence, scope, or next action.

## Discussion prompts

- When does a source’s commercial interest matter?
- Can internal testing be stronger than a formal external publication?
- What is the difference between missing evidence and negative evidence?
- Why can “more research needed” still be an inadequate conclusion?

## Minimum acceptable evidence of learning

The matrix identifies provenance and comparability; the memo contains a bounded recommendation, explicit uncertainty, and specific next evidence; repeated claims are not treated as independent support.

## Extension

Ask the student to design one fair comparison test that would reduce the most important uncertainty. They should specify samples, conditions, measurements, and decision threshold.

## Fallback plan

If time is short, complete the matrix for six high-value claims rather than every sentence, but still require at least one repeated claim, one non-comparable measurement, one cost-scope issue, and one evidence gap.
