# AF-CAP-101 — Capstone Instructor Guide

## Purpose

The capstone tests whether the student can combine AI tools with disciplined professional judgment. It is not a test of producing the most files or the most polished visuals.

## Recommended schedule

Run the capstone over four to seven days.

1. **Kickoff and evidence map** — define the decision, constraints, and evidence gaps.
2. **Research and data build** — verify external sources and create the workbook.
3. **Plan, agent, and CAD validation** — develop bounded operational and technical artifacts.
4. **Leadership review** — present the recommendation, defend assumptions, and respond to a new constraint.

## Instructor role

- Do not resolve contradictions for the student.
- Ask where each important number or claim came from.
- Require the student to label assumptions before using them.
- Introduce one controlled change after the first recommendation, such as a lower budget ceiling, unavailable supplier, or failed fit coupon.
- Evaluate whether the student updates connected artifacts consistently.

## Strong submission characteristics

A strong submission:

- begins with a precise decision rather than a general product description;
- preserves uncertainty and source limitations;
- uses AI outputs as drafts, analyses, or alternatives rather than authority;
- has formulas and parameters that respond correctly to changed assumptions;
- links every major risk to a test, owner, and exit criterion;
- keeps monitoring and irreversible action separate;
- distinguishes concept CAD from validated engineering;
- makes a recommendation that can be weaker than “proceed.”

## Defensible recommendation patterns

More than one conclusion may be correct. The most likely strong recommendation is a **conditional, bounded pre-pilot or engineering-validation phase**, because the supplied evidence does not establish complete cost, validated demand, final geometry, supplier readiness, regulatory compliance, or field readiness.

A recommendation to revise or stop can also earn full credit when it follows the evidence and states what would reverse the decision.

## Required challenge questions

Ask the student:

1. Which single assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation?
2. What evidence would make you reverse the recommendation?
3. Which AI-generated statement was most convincing but least supported?
4. Where do two artifacts use the same fact differently?
5. What action is intentionally left to a human?
6. What does the CAD model prove, and what does it not prove?
7. How would your plan change if the pilot quantity were halved?

## Controlled-change exercise

After the initial presentation, provide one change:

- budget ceiling reduced by 20%;
- preferred supplier becomes unavailable;
- customer host withdraws;
- fit coupon fails cable-clearance check;
- assembly-time trial is 40% slower than estimated.

Give the student 30–45 minutes to update the recommendation, workbook, risk register, plan, and slides. The key assessment is consistency, not speed.

## Red flags

Reduce credit for:

- unsupported certainty;
- copied AI prose the student cannot explain;
- citations not checked against source claims;
- hard-coded calculations that fail when quantity changes;
- a polished deck that hides unknowns;
- agent designs that purchase or approve automatically;
- CAD claims exceeding render or fit-test evidence;
- contradictory assumptions across deliverables;
- missing decision ownership or reversal conditions.

## Completion decision

Award the course promotion to **Lead Applied AI Engineer** when the student can defend the final recommendation, identify its weaknesses, and demonstrate a traceable revision after the controlled change.
