# EDU-CAP-099 — Step-by-Step Capstone Facilitation Script

**Suggested duration:** 4–6 sessions, 7–11 hours total.

**Instructor stance:** Ask for traceability and teacher reasoning; do not supply the instructional conclusion.

## 1. Kickoff: choose a bounded context (30–45 min)

Have the learner select one target band and explain the central inquiry. Review the rule that the plan must still show adaptations for all four bands. Confirm that all examples and gradebook data are fictional/public and that teacher judgment remains final.

Prompt: “Which choice is evidence-based, which is constrained by the classroom, and which is your professional judgment?”

## 2. Evidence and constraint map (45–60 min)

Review the evidence register before drafting slides. Require four entries with source type, limitation, local verification, and affected artifacts. Ask the learner to mark a source that should not be treated as authority, such as an AI or vendor claim.

Checkpoint: no consequential claim may appear in the sequence, rubric, deck, or family draft without an evidence ID, a constraint, or a labelled teacher judgment.

## 3. Design the learning arc and differentiation (75–100 min)

The learner drafts the ten-day sequence, formative checks, assessment, and four-band differentiation. Ask how K–2 participation, 3–5 evidence explanation, 6–8 source evaluation, and 9–12 argument change the same inquiry. Inspect access and expression options; do not accept a lower-thinking substitute as differentiation.

## 4. Build and review communication artifacts (60–90 min)

Review the neutral fictional-gradebook pattern note, family draft, and six-slide deck. Ask the learner to trace two slide claims to sources, read alt text aloud, and identify the approval required before a family message could be sent. Reject unsupported local facts, decorative but misleading visualizations, and grading language that treats a pattern as a conclusion.

## 5. Optional extension boundary check (20–30 min)

If selected, inspect the resource hub or digest specification. It must remain local/design-only, avoid student data and data collection, log or document intended behavior, and require teacher approval before sharing. Do not permit deployment, automated messaging, grade actions, placement, behavioral decisions, or interventions.

## 6. Controlled challenge and final defense (45–60 min)

Introduce one change: a shorter lesson period, unavailable investigation material, a family translation need, or a source found to be weak. Require an impact analysis before revision. Then ask the learner to defend one evidence choice, one accessibility choice, one privacy boundary, and one decision AI could not make.

Record the review in the decision log. A polished deck cannot compensate for missing traceability or human review.
