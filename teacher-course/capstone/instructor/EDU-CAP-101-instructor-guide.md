# EDU-CAP-101 — Capstone Instructor Reference Guide

## Strong submission characteristics

A strong submission makes a bounded, workable teaching plan rather than claiming a universally correct one. It:

- links consequential choices to the evidence register, a constraint, or explicit teacher judgment;
- distinguishes fictional research/observation/guidance from local verified facts;
- makes the water inquiry visible in a ten-day sequence and across all four grade bands;
- uses formative evidence to inform a teacher review, not automatic grades or labels;
- includes a rubric and deck that learners can access without relying only on colour, tiny text, or unsupported visuals;
- protects privacy and reserves family communication, grades, interventions, and safeguarding decisions for people;
- documents verification, revisions, uncertainty, and a meaningful AI limitation.

## Acceptable instructional conclusions

There is no single correct local example, assessment product, or family message. Full credit can be earned by different approaches if they remain evidence-bound, age-appropriate, accessible, and feasible. Do not reward a more technologically elaborate plan over a safer, better-supported one.

## Required challenge questions

1. Which source supports this claim, and what does it fail to establish?
2. Which classroom constraint changed your sequence?
3. How does the K–2 move preserve the same central thinking as the 9–12 move?
4. What could a fictional gradebook pattern suggest, and what may it never decide?
5. How can a learner access this slide without colour or a visual image?
6. What must the teacher verify before this family draft is sent?
7. Which AI output did you reject, revise, or leave unresolved?
8. If the optional digest/hub existed, what action must remain behind teacher approval?

## Controlled-change options

Give one change after a first draft: the investigation materials cannot be used; the schedule loses one class period; a key source lacks adequate support; or the family communication requires a new language/access format. The learner first records which claims, files, and decisions are affected, then updates only the connected work. Look for stale slide claims, inaccessible alternatives, or a lost privacy boundary.

## Red flags

Reduce credit or require revision when the learner:

- presents AI confidence as evidence;
- treats scores/missingness as learner ability, behavior, placement, or intervention evidence;
- provides identical differentiation for every grade band;
- omits alt text, caption planning, readable contrast, or non-colour cues;
- uploads or invents real student/family information;
- allows a workflow to send, publish, grade, or decide without teacher approval;
- cannot trace a major claim to a source, constraint, or documented judgment.

## Completion decision

The learner meets the capstone standard when they can defend a coherent K–12 water-unit package, show cross-artifact traceability, revise after a challenge, and explain exactly where teacher verification and judgment remain necessary.
