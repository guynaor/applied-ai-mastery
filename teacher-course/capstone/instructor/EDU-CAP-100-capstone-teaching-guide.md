# EDU-CAP-100 — Capstone Teaching Guide

## Purpose

This capstone assesses whether a teacher can use AI as a bounded design partner while retaining responsibility for learning, evidence, accessibility, privacy, and communication. It is not a request for an autonomous teaching system or a generic unit-plan download.

## Teaching objectives

The learner demonstrates that they can:

1. connect sources and classroom constraints to consequential choices;
2. create a coherent water-unit sequence for a selected band while differentiating across K–12;
3. evaluate evidence quality, uncertainty, and local fit;
4. make instructional communication accessible and age-appropriate;
5. interpret fictional patterns as questions for teacher review, never automated conclusions;
6. explain the boundary between an AI draft and a teacher decision.

## Recommended flow

Use the facilitation script in four to six meetings: kickoff/evidence map; sequence and differentiation; assessment/data/communication artifacts; cross-artifact review; controlled challenge and defense. Require a review checkpoint before finalizing the deck and again before final submission.

## What to inspect

### Traceability

Select five consequential choices—such as a local example, source, grouping approach, assessment criterion, family-message claim, or deck visual. The learner must locate each in the evidence register or decision log, state the constraint/assumption, and identify a verification method. Missing traceability is a revision issue even if prose is polished.

### Differentiation and accessibility

Check that all four bands change the learning move appropriately, rather than merely simplifying vocabulary. Look for readable contrast, alt text/captions, non-colour cues, keyboard-ready interaction when an app is chosen, language supports, and multiple legitimate response modes.

### Privacy and professional boundaries

Confirm that data are fictional/public, missing values are preserved, and no artifact asks AI to grade, profile, place, discipline, select an intervention, send a message, or make safeguarding decisions. Family drafts require teacher/policy/translation review before any real use.

### Verification and human judgment

Ask what the evidence does not establish, what must be checked locally, and what the teacher changed or rejected from AI. A suitable answer names concrete review steps, not only “I would double-check it.”

## Common difficulties and interventions

**Starting with a beautiful deck.** Return the learner to the evidence register and lesson constraints.

**One-size-fits-all differentiation.** Ask learners to demonstrate how each band handles evidence and expression differently while preserving the inquiry.

**Treating a gradebook pattern as diagnosis.** Require neutral wording, original-work review, and a teacher-selected low-stakes response.

**Calling a digest or hub an agent deployment.** Keep it a local design/specification with no real data, external connection, or sending authority.

## Assessment discussion

Ask the learner to defend: one source they did not trust fully; one change they made for accessibility; one claim removed after verification; and one action deliberately reserved for the teacher. Score reasoning, traceability, and safe implementation over volume or visual polish.
