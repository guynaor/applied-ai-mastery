# EDU-CAP-001 — Teacher-Reviewed K–12 Water Unit Plan

**Role:** Teacher and final instructional decision-maker

**Scenario:** Adapt the fictional two-week *Water in Our Shared World* unit for one selected grade band, while showing how the same central inquiry changes for K–2, 3–5, 6–8, and 9–12.

## Central inquiry

How do people use, value, and protect shared water? Choose a local connection only after the teacher verifies its accuracy, safety, relevance, and community context.

## Required deliverables

### 1. Traceable lesson sequence

Create a feasible ten-school-day sequence. Include an investigation or observation, source-evaluation moment, data representation, formative checks, final performance/communication task, materials, timing, and teacher checkpoints. Trace constraints such as schedule, access needs, safety, language supports, and assessment dates to the choices you make.

### 2. Four-band differentiation plan

Design one coherent learning arc and show a meaningful version for every band:

| Grade band | Expected learning move |
|---|---|
| K–2 | Observe water, use a read-aloud or image sequence, sort evidence with oral/visual response options. |
| 3–5 | Investigate a local water question, interpret a simple table or graph, and explain a claim with evidence. |
| 6–8 | Evaluate source reliability, model patterns in data, and propose a supported action. |
| 9–12 | Compare claims and trade-offs, evaluate methods/uncertainty, and make a civic argument with rebuttal. |

Adjust access, participation, language, and expression while preserving the central thinking. Do not label a learner or make placement decisions from AI output.

### 3. Evidence-based content choice

Use the evidence register to record at least four sources or supplied course artifacts. Distinguish research, district/local guidance, observation, and vendor/AI claims. For every consequential claim, record what the source supports, its limit, and how the teacher will verify local fit.

### 4. Assessment plan and rubric

Create a learner-facing or teacher-use assessment rubric aligned to the unit goal. It needs clear success criteria, an accessible way to demonstrate learning, and a plan for feedback before the final task. AI may draft language, but it must not assign grades, infer behavior, determine placement, or select interventions.

### 5. Fictional gradebook-pattern analysis

Use only the fictional data from Mission 3. Preserve missing values, apply formula-based cleaning, and write a neutral pattern note: what the table suggests, what the teacher must check, and what it does **not** establish. Propose a low-stakes instructional follow-up; the teacher reviews all outcomes.

### 6. Family communication draft

Draft a short, translation-ready family message explaining the inquiry, dates/materials, accessible participation options, and a way to contact the teacher. Do not request private household information. The teacher checks language, local policy, translation, and distribution before sending.

### 7. Six-slide teaching deck

Create six slides: (1) inquiry and relevance, (2) what learners notice, (3) evidence/source practice, (4) data or investigation, (5) discussion/action choices, (6) reflection or performance task. Provide source notes, speaker notes, captions/alt text, readable contrast, and a non-colour-only way to read meaning. Claims and visuals must be age-appropriate and supported.

### 8. Reflection and review

Explain where AI helped, where it created risk or uncertainty, what you rejected or changed, and which decisions required teacher judgment. Update the decision log after a peer/instructor challenge.

## Optional extension — design only

Choose **one**:

- adapt the local, dependency-free classroom resource hub from Mission 7 using fictional/public materials and no data collection; or
- specify a bounded weekly planning digest that monitors fictional public curriculum snapshots, logs changes, suppresses duplicates, stops safely, excludes student data, and requires teacher approval before any sharing.

Do not deploy, publish, send messages, collect data, or connect the extension to real school systems.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Use fictional, public, or teacher-approved de-identified materials only.
- Verify standards, local facts, accessibility needs, safety expectations, family-language needs, and policy before use.
- Label AI outputs, assumptions, estimates, and unresolved questions.
- The teacher retains final responsibility for instructional choices, grading, communication, safeguarding, and any external action.
