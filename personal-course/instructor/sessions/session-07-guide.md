# Mentor guide — One Project, Every Skill

**Format:** A home project with asynchronous mentoring in the group chat. There is no class to run.
**Learning outcome:** Learners carry one subject of their own through five deliverables, each re-using a session, and finish with a working app whose boundary they wrote themselves.
**Artifact:** One project: research brief, spreadsheet plan, presentation, designed object, small app, and the app's boundary.

**This replaces the seventh session.** Nothing is scheduled and nothing is projected. Your work here is answering in the group thread, and the whole model rests on one thing: that people post.

## The subject gate — your main job

Everything else in this guide is smaller than this. A learner who starts on a bad subject spends weeks and arrives at deliverable four with a designed object they had to invent a reason for. That is not recoverable by good feedback later; it is only preventable here.

**The test, asked of each proposed subject:** does each of the five deliverables arise from this naturally? Read their proposal and try to name, concretely, what the object would be and what the app would do. If you cannot — or if the honest answer is "a spreadsheet, but called an app" — the subject is wrong.

### Three judgements, and what to say

**"A weekend trip to the north."** Accept. Research is real comparison, the plan is an itinerary and budget, the presentation goes to whoever is coming, the object is something that goes in the bag, and the app is a packing list they will reuse. Reply naming the five so they can see the shape: *"This works — I can see all five: costs to compare, an itinerary, showing it to whoever's coming, something for the bag, and a packing list you'll use again. What's your end date?"*

**"Learn to play the guitar."** Send back. Say which deliverables do not arise, not just "no": *"The research is real and the practice log would be a genuine app. But the plan is a wish rather than a schedule with costs, nobody needs the presentation, and the object would be invented to satisfy the requirement. Narrow it — 'set up a corner where practising actually happens' gives you a real stand or cable tidy, and a presentation that argues for the space."* The brief carries this same example; pointing at it is fine.

**"Redo my CV."** Send back, and be direct about why: there is no object, no plan with costs, and the app is imaginary. This one comes up because it feels useful. Useful is not the test.

### Two things to get at the gate

- **Their own end date.** Nothing else anchors this — there is no next class. Ask for it in the same message you confirm the subject, and repeat it back.
- **Which deliverable they are skipping**, if they missed the session it draws on.

## What an answer contains

Every answer is read by everyone, so each one teaches more people than it addresses. That is the whole reason this runs in the open — and it is why "looks good" is a waste of the seat.

Three parts, every time:

1. **What it got right**, specifically enough that they know which part to keep doing.
2. **The one thing that would most improve it.** One. A list gets skimmed.
3. **Done, or coming back.** Say which.

## Reviewing each deliverable

The standards already exist. For each, the session's own rubric applies, and the failure below is the one you will actually see.

| Deliverable | Standard from | The failure to expect |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Research brief** | Session 2 | Sources are all one vendor's marketing, and assumptions are written as findings |
| **2. Plan with a spreadsheet** | Session 3 | A schedule with no cost, no owner, and nothing about what happens when it slips |
| **3. Presentation** | Session 4 | Generated material unlabelled — or worse, presented as a record of something real |
| **4. Designed object** | Session 5 | A dimension with no source, and no real measurement ever taken |
| **5. Small app** | Session 6 | It works, and it has no boundary. Not finished. |

**Deliverable 5 is where the safety material lives now.** It is not done until the learner has written what the app may touch, what always needs their approval, and what stops it. If they send the app without the boundary, that is a "coming back", not a nitpick.

## What the group thread costs and what it buys

Answering in the open is what stops your effort scaling with the size of the group. The answer to "your sources are all one vendor" is worth more to the five people who have not posted yet than to the one who did, and you write it once.

**The failure mode is silence.** If nobody posts, this quietly becomes private mentoring at full cost with none of the compounding. Watch the first subject gate — if nothing appears there, it will not appear later either. Post your own subject first and answer it yourself; a thread with something in it is much easier to join than an empty one.

## Quick assessment rubric

| Evidence | Not yet | Ready |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Chosen alone, work already started | Proposed, confirmed, with an end date they set |
| Research | Sources that agree with each other | Opened themselves, with what is still unknown named |
| Plan | A list of steps | Costs, owners, and what happens when it slips |
| Presentation | Looks finished | Everything generated is labelled |
| Object | A drawing | Parametric, sourced dimensions, checked against a real measurement |
| App | It runs | It runs, and it knows what it may not do |

## Troubleshooting

| Situation | Mentor response |
|---|---|
| Nobody posts at the subject gate | The model depends on posting. Post your own subject and answer it publicly — an empty thread is the hardest one to join |
| A subject fails the five-deliverable test | Send it back now, not at deliverable four. Name which deliverables do not arise, and suggest a narrower version |
| A learner missed the session a deliverable draws on | They skip it. A shorter project honestly done beats a guessed deliverable |
| Someone has gone quiet past their own end date | Ask once, in the group, without judgement. The date was theirs, not yours |
| Someone posts work containing private information | Say so immediately and publicly. The group is watching, and that is the lesson |
| An app arrives with no boundary | Coming back. The boundary is the deliverable, not paperwork attached to it |
| Someone wants to change subject midway | Fine before deliverable three, expensive after. Re-run the gate on the new subject |

## Differentiation

- **A learner who missed several sessions:** fewer deliverables, same standard on the ones they do. Do not let them attempt a deliverable for a session they were not in.
- **A learner who finishes early:** ask them to answer someone else's post before starting anything new. It is the cheapest way to raise the whole group.
- **A learner who wants a private thread:** allow it, but say what they lose. Most requests come from not wanting to post something rough; saying that rough is normal usually resolves it.

## After the projects come in

Note which deliverable produced the most "coming back" answers — that is the session to strengthen next time. If the subject gate rejected several subjects for the same reason, put that reason into the brief's counter-example before the next cohort.
