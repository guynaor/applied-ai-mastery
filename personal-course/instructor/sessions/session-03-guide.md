# Full session plan — Session 3: Make a Shared Plan Work

**Time:** 55–60 minutes
**Learning outcome:** Learners carry out five real event-organising jobs with AI, and after each one identify what the tool assumed, what it invented, and who actually owns it.
**Session artifact:** One page you could send to whoever is helping: food and drink quantities with assumptions marked, a link to a running image display, a run sheet with a source for every changing fact, a task table with names and dates, and one fallback.

This guide can be taught from directly. The session is written **remote-first**: every activity works for a learner sitting alone at a laptop, and anyone in the room does exactly the same thing.

## Before learners arrive

### Preparation — remote and in the room

1. Open the [slides](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) for screen sharing, and the [planning sources](../../instructor/samples/session-03-sources.html) in a separate tab.
2. Prepare **one shared document** everyone can write in — this is where "circulating the room" now happens. Put five headings in it: food and drinks, image display, timing, dividing work, what breaks.
3. Have the [disruption cards](../../instructor/samples/session-03-cards.html) link ready to paste into the chat — and do not send it early.
4. Check that **Claude Artifacts** is available in the facilitator account for the image display demonstration.

### What learners need

- One real event they are organising. If they have none, the 40-person family event from the sample sources.
- A device with one AI tool. In this session the tool *is* the work, so anyone without one joins a pair in a breakout room or on a call.
- Access to the shared document.

### Two facilitator decisions before starting

**Who works with whom.** Remotely, breakout pairs work better than one large group — one person runs the tool while the other asks the three questions. In the room, a pair at one screen. Do not leave anyone alone with the tool and nobody to challenge the output.

**How long on the image display.** It is the long block at 13 minutes, and the only one that produces something learners take away. If the session is running late, shorten dividing the work — not the display.

## Session map

| Time | The AI job | The check that follows |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | Opening: what are you organising | One real event |
| 5–15 | Food and drinks: quantities and a shopping list | What did it assume about you? |
| 15–28 | Image display in Claude Artifacts | Two checks, and the consent question |
| 28–38 | Timing | Which facts change? Source and date |
| 38–50 | Dividing the work | "Someone should" becomes a name and a date |
| 50–60 | Disruption card and fallback | What happens, who triggers it, by when |

## 0–5 minutes — Opening

Show [slide 1](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) and ask: "What are you organising right now that needs other people to happen?" Remotely, ask for one answer each in the chat. It is faster than a round of speaking and leaves a written list.

Then show [slide 2](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) — the three questions that return five times today: what did it **assume**, what did it **invent**, and who **owns** it.

### What to say

> "Today we are not learning a planning method. We are doing the work itself, and after each job we ask the same three questions. If those are all you remember from this session, that is enough."

## 5–15 minutes — Food and drinks

Show the request on [slide 3](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) and run it. Note the last sentence: **"separate what I told you from what you assumed yourself."** Without it you get numbers with no idea where they came from.

### Expected intermediate output

Quantities and a shopping list, followed by a list of assumptions. If there is no list of assumptions, re-run with only that final sentence.

### The moment that matters

Show [slide 4](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html): one and a half portions per person, two drinks an hour, children eating like adults, guests staying to the end. The tool settled all of these and never asked.

### What to say

> "Notice that none of those assumptions is wrong. They are simply not yours. The gap between one and one and a half portions per person is real money, and it was decided by something that has no idea how much people actually eat at your events."

## 15–28 minutes — The image display

The long block, and the only one that produces something that will run at the event itself. Show the request on [slide 5](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) and run it in Claude Artifacts.

### Expected intermediate output

One page cycling placeholder images with captions, a pause control, and speed control. If it came back as an app with uploads or an account, the request was too broad.

### The two checks

Show [slide 6](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html): what happens with **only two images**, and with **a very long caption**. Those two almost always find something.

### And the question that arrives on its own

**Whose photos are these, and who agreed to appear in them.** Do not teach this as a lecture — just ask it of whoever is already deciding which pictures to use.

## 28–38 minutes — Timing

Give the tool the [planning sources](../../instructor/samples/session-03-sources.html) — the page address, or the text from the button at the top — and ask for a run sheet.

Then show [slide 7](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html): which facts change, and a source and date for each.

### The three conflicts

They are already in the sources and should surface now: the hall available until 22:00 with vacation by 22:30 against a plan stretching to 23:00; the last bus at 22:40; the caterer closing numbers 10 days out against a printer needing 7 working days.

### If the tool flagged no conflict at all

Ask the class: "Until when is the hall open, and until when does our event run?" Let them find the first one, then ask the tool to check again. That teaches more than if it had found them itself.

## 38–50 minutes — Dividing the work

Ask for tasks split with owners and dates, and show [slide 8](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html).

The tool will write "someone should send invitations". That is the line to fix: **a name and a date**. Everyone corrects three rows in the shared document — so everyone sees everyone else's corrections, remote and in the room alike.

### What to say

> "What the tool cannot know: who is genuinely free this week, and who has already taken on too much. That is not a gap in the tool — that is your part."

## 50–60 minutes — What breaks

**Now** paste the [disruption cards](../../instructor/samples/session-03-cards.html) link into the chat. Each learner picks a card.

Show [slide 9](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html): a fallback needs what happens instead, who triggers it, and by when it must be decided.

Then [slide 10](../../instructor/samples/session-03-slides.html) for the exit check:

> The tool saved me **[what]**. Its assumption that surprised me most was **[what]**. The task that now has a name is **[what]**, and it belongs to **[who]** by **[when]**.

Reflection question: "What did the tool do better than you — and what did you do better than it?"

### Quick assessment rubric

| Evidence | Not yet | Ready |
|---|---|---|
| Assumptions | They got quantities | Assumptions listed separately from facts |
| The display | "It looks fine" | Tested with two images and with a long caption |
| Changing facts | "The hall is open late" | A source and date for every fact that changes |
| Ownership | "Invitations need sending" | "Aunt, by 1 October" |
| Fallback | "We'll see what happens" | What, who, and by when |

## Equivalent tool routes

**Claude:** Artifacts for the image display, and the same requests for everything else. The most comfortable route today.
**ChatGPT / Gemini:** Exactly the same requests. For the display, ask for one HTML file and open it locally.
**A tool that cannot open links:** The copy button at the top of the sources page. No gap.
**No tool:** Work from the planning sources on paper and fill in the same tables. The checks are identical.

## Troubleshoot without losing the lesson

| Situation | Facilitator response |
|---|---|
| A learner's tool will not open the link | The "copy all sources as text" button at the top of the sources page. |
| Someone opened the disruption cards early | Not a disaster. Give them a different card, or ask them to take the least convenient one. |
| Artifacts is unavailable | Ask for one HTML file and open it locally. |
| The display came back overloaded | Remind them: an image, a caption, a pause. Three things. |
| A learner wants to upload real photos of people | Not today, and not without those people knowing. Placeholders. |
| The tool invented times or prices | Stop and point at it. Move it to "assumption". This is the heart of the session. |
| Time runs out | Shorten dividing the work. Do not drop the disruption card. |

## Differentiation

- **Fully remote:** The whole session was designed this way. No activity requires a room.
- **No device:** Join a pair. Whoever has no tool is the one asking the three questions — the more important role.
- **For learners who need structure:** Give them the sample event from the sources and ask them to change only the dates and names.
- **For learners ready to extend:** Ask them to find the fourth conflict — the caterer clears within an hour of the end, and the hall requires full vacation by 22:30.

## After the session

Note which of the tool's assumptions most surprised the group — it will probably recur, and is worth adding to slide 4. If anyone ran the image display at a real event, ask them to report back next session on what happened when it ran in front of people.
