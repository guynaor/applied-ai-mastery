Slide 1 — Opening, minutes 0–5

What are you organising right now that needs someone else to make it happen?

A good plan is not the one that looks tidiest. It is the one someone else can pick up and run without calling you to ask what you meant.

Slide 2 — Constraint vs preference, minutes 5–15

Two columns, and most mistakes happen on the border between them

Constraint — cannot movePreference — can flex
The hall is booked for 7 NovemberFamily-style seating at tables
Budget of 18,000Short speeches only
Grandma needs step-free accessLive music rather than a playlist

And the line that settles it

Who decides when preferences collide? One name. Not “we'll talk about it”.

Slide 3 — The facts that change, minutes 15–25

Every fact gets a source and a date

Opening hours  •  travel times  •  prices  •  cancellation terms  •  booking cut-offs

These are exactly the details that were true last year and are not true now. “I knew that” is not a source.

Slide 4 — The request, minutes 25–40

The tool arranges. It does not invent.

Arrange this event using only the facts below. Do not add any detail I did not give you, and do not invent times, prices, or availability. Put what is fixed first, then tasks, travel, and breaks. Give every row an owner and a date, and add a 30-minute buffer between stages. Separately flag every place where the facts I gave you contradict each other, and anything missing that I need in order to finish the plan.

Slide 5 — What the tool should find

Three conflicts already sitting in the facts

The venue: available until 22:00, fully vacated by 22:30. But our plan has the event running to 23:00.
Route 47: last bus at 22:40. Anyone who came by public transport has to leave before the event ends.
The caterer: closes numbers 10 days before. Printed invitations take 7 working days after design approval.
If the tool did not flag them, that does not mean they are not there. It means we asked it to arrange, and it arranged.

Slide 7 — The disruption card, minutes 40–50

Now we break it

One card. Not a scenario you prepared for — something that actually happens.

  • Heavy rain between 17:00 and 19:00, and the yard parking floods.
  • Twelve days left, eight guests unconfirmed, caterer closes in two days.
  • Whoever runs the music is ill, two days before.
  • Street works will block access to the yard that evening.

Slide 8 — What a fallback must contain

Three things, or it is not a plan

  1. What happens instead — one clear action.
  2. Who triggers it — a name, not “someone”.
  3. By when it must be decided — the moment after which there is no choice left.
“We'll see what happens” is not a fallback. It is postponing the decision to the moment it becomes more expensive.

Complete out loud or in writing

My plan breaks if [the disruption]. In that case [who] will do [what], and the decision has to be made by [when]. Before I send anything to people, I will recheck [one fact].

And the reflection question

Which of your preferences was disguised as a constraint?
