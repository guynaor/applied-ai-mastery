02 · Where numbers come fromMinutes 15–30

Every number has a source

Measured

You put a ruler on it. Say where you put it.

Spec

From the maker’s sheet. True of the product, maybe not of yours.

Assumed

Nobody checked. These are the ones that cost you.

Calculated

Derived. Only ever as good as what went in.

This is the part the AI cannot do for you. It has not seen the room.

04 · The forgotten oneMinutes 15–30

“The drawer opens fully”

If the runners stop at 80 per cent, the usable depth is 33.6 cm, not 42.

One unchecked assumption, and every compartment behind it is wrong. This is why the label matters more than the number.

05 · The requestMinutes 30–55

Give it real numbers, with their labels

The full request
These are measurements I took, each with a source label. All in millimetres. Build a parametric organiser that fits inside them. Then list every dimension you used that I did not give you.

06 · What it filled inMinutes 30–55

“Which dimensions did you use that I never gave you?”

Wall thickness. Clearance. Corner radius. Base height.

None of them is necessarily wrong. They were simply never measured — the same assumption-that-looks-like-a-fact, wearing a different hat.

07 · Predict, then changeMinutes 55–70

5 compartments
68.7 mm each
7 compartments
predict it, out loud, first
Then check

48.4 mm. If your prediction missed, you did not understand the model — better to find that now than after a three-hour print.

09 · The limitMinutes 70–85

What we are not designing

  1. Nothing that carries a person’s weight.
  2. Nothing involving electrics.
  3. Nothing that is dangerous if it fails.
