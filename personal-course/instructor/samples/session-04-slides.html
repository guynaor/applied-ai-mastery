Slide 1 — Opening, minutes 0–5

What do you redo on a scrap of paper every month, and always lose?

Today we build one small tool. Success is not that it looks good — it is that you know exactly where it breaks.

Slide 2 — Shrink it, minutes 5–15

One user, one problem, at most three actions

User and problem: I split shared costs with two flatmates every month.

Three actions: add an expense  •  show balances  •  settle up.

What it must not do: store bank details, or move real money.

Every fourth action you add costs you a test you will not have time to run.

Slide 3 — The request, minutes 15–30

Ask for one tool, not an app

Build one self-contained web page for splitting costs between three flatmates. Three actions only: add an expense (who paid, how much, what for), show how much each person owes, and reset. Plain labels, a clear reset button, everything in a single file. No login, no connection to an external service, and no sending data anywhere. If a field is empty or not a number, show a helpful message instead of calculating.

Slide 4 — Before touching the tool

Three questions about the code you were handed

  1. Where is the data stored — and what happens on refresh?
  2. What does it do with input you did not expect?
  3. Does anything here leave the page and go to the internet?
Code generated in thirty seconds is not safe merely because it runs.

Slide 7 — The moment that matters

A card that broke the tool is a test that succeeded

We are not looking for a tool that works. We are looking for its limit — because without it you will not know when to stop trusting it.

Then

Record what happened  →  fix one thing  →  test again.

Slide 8 — Control, minutes 42–50

Four decisions before you send it to anyone

The questionExample
Where is the tool stored?One file on my own machine, not in the cloud
Who can open it?Me, and later my two flatmates
How is data cleared?A reset button, and closing the page clears it anyway
When do you stop using it?If we move to a real app, or if it gets money wrong once

Slide 9 — What we are not building today

The limit of this tool

  • No passwords and no account details
  • No other people's data without their knowing
  • No decisions with financial or medical weight
Lovable and Replit are a next step for a larger or collaborative project — not needed today, and no sign-up required.

Slide 10 — Exit check, minutes 50–60

Complete out loud or in writing

My tool does [one action]. It breaks when [the edge case], so [the fix or the warning]. The data lives in [where] and is cleared by [how].

And the reflection question

What does the tool do better than the scrap of paper — and what did the paper do better than it?
