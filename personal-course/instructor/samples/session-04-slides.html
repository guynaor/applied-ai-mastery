01 · The three rulesMinutes 10–16

Three rules, stated once, applied all session

  1. Label it. Anything generated is said out loud, in the caption.
  2. No real people without consent. Not even from a public place.
  3. Never a record. Generated work does not stand in for something that happened.

Six stages from here. Ask all three again at every one — the repetition is the safety lesson.

02 · The chainMinutes 10–16

One subject, six stages, one artifact

One subject travels the whole way. Nothing is swapped partway — stage six presents whatever stages one to five actually produced.

03 · Prompt anatomyMinutes 16–27

Five things a prompt has to say

Subject
what is actually in the frame
Composition
the angle and distance it is seen from
Light
the source, the direction, the time of day
Framing
where the empty space sits, for a title later
Exclusions
what must not appear

04 · Weak against strongMinutes 16–27

The same picture, asked for twice

“A nice photo of a garden.”

And the same thing, said properly
A community garden bed in full bloom, seen at eye level from the path, late afternoon light from the left, empty sky in the upper third for a title, no people, no text, no logos.

05 · Two toolsMinutes 16–27

Same prompt, two tools

Exclusions
which one actually left out what you told it to?
Invention
which one added something you never asked for?
Match
which is closer to what you actually asked for?

Not a contest between products. A way of seeing what your own prompt left open.

06 · Fix, do not re-rollMinutes 27–38

Aim at the patch that failed

Re-rolling gives you a different picture. Fixing gives you your picture, repaired.

Same tool as stage one. Point the edit at the region that broke, not at the whole frame again from nothing.

07 · Move itMinutes 38–46

The prompt steers motion. It does not invent the subject.

Leaves sway gently in a light breeze. Camera holds still. Nothing else in frame changes.

This is still your image. The model is moving it, not replacing it.

08 · Where video breaksMinutes 46–56

Four places it still falls apart

Text
signage and labels come out wrong more often than right
Hands
count the fingers before you trust the frame
Continuity
what is true in frame one may not hold by frame four
Physics
weight, water and shadows drift

Shoot short. Four seconds that hold together beats fifteen that fall apart halfway.

09 · The callbackMinutes 46–56

Would you publish either one about a real place?

Both clips are generated. Nothing here was shot with a camera.

The answer, once they have argued about it

Neither is a record of that place, so no. A label does not make it true — it stops it deceiving. Rule three is the one people forget first.

10 · Write for the earMinutes 56–65

Write for the ear, not the eye

Sentences
short — if it needs a comma to survive, split it
One idea
per breath. Not three joined with “and”
Numbers
spoken: “about twelve thousand”, not “12,847”

The script matters more than the voice. Fix it on the page before you generate anything.

11 · Two formatsMinutes 65–85

Same tool, two formats

PPTX

Opens in PowerPoint or Slides. Anyone can drag a slide around. A binary you cannot read as text.

HTML

One file, any browser, nothing installed. Plain text you can read and diff. Editing means editing markup.

The comparison is the lesson, not the tool — it is the same tool both times.

12 · Exit checkMinutes 85–90

Finish the sentence

My subject was . It got through of the six stages. The stage that surprised me was .

And the question worth sitting with

Which of the three rules would be easiest to break by accident?
