Slide 1 — Opening, minutes 0–5

A useful AI answer is not a decision

Today we build one decision page. By the end you will know not only what you chose, but on what basis — and what you still have not checked.

Slide 2 — AI Geography, minutes 5–17

Six layers, and one control point in each

LayerIn plain termsWhere we keep control
Large language modelThe engine that produces the answerCheck important claims at the source
AI applicationThe interface: Claude, ChatGPT, GeminiCheck which capabilities are active

Slide 4 — The vague request, minutes 23–30

What would the tool have to guess?

“Which Saturday activity should I choose?”

Date  •  budget  •  time available  •  distance  •  what interests you  •  who decides

Anything we did not say, the tool will fill in itself — and with complete confidence.

Slide 5 — The built request

Five parts: goal, context, constraints, output, check

I am choosing between the Saturday gardening class and the museum talk described below. I have 3 hours, a budget of 100, and want a social activity. Use only the supplied source. Create a table with: verified facts, my preferences, unknowns, and claims I must check. Then compare the two options. Do not choose for me, do not invent availability, and label uncertainty.

Slide 6 — The four labels

Not everything belongs on the same line

LabelExample from the page
Verified factThe class runs 10:00–12:00, costs 45, booking required by Thursday
Preference“I want a social activity”
Assumption“There will probably be space without booking”
UnknownWhether a friend is free, and how long the journey takes

Slide 7 — Visible verification, minutes 30–40

The tidy table is a draft, not a source

Check two claims only — the ones that would change the decision if they turned out to be wrong.

We go back to the original page because a decision changes when a fact changes. A confident answer is not evidence.

Slide 8 — When the output is weak

Three failures and what to say

What happenedWhat to say
It invented a detail not on the page“Where did that come from?” and move it to “unknown”
The tool chose for usReturn to “do not choose for me” and re-run
It only shortened the page“Which facts affect a decision, and what still needs verifying?”

Slide 9 — What stays human judgment

What the tool cannot decide for you

  • What actually matters to you
  • How much uncertainty you are willing to absorb
  • Whether to act now or gather more information
The tool organises. You decide. That is not a polite formula — it is the division of labour for the whole course.

Slide 10 — Exit check, minutes 52–60

Complete out loud or in writing

I will do [next step] because the verified facts are [two facts], but I still need to check [unknown] by [recheck date].

And the reflection question

Which preference or missing context could the tool not decide for you?
