06 · The vague askMinutes 34–40

What would it have to guess?

“Which Saturday activity should I choose?”

Date · budget · time you have · distance · what interests you · who decides

Anything you did not say, it will fill in for you — with complete confidence.

07 · The built askMinutes 40–45

Goal, context, constraints, output, check

The full request
I am choosing between the Saturday gardening class and the museum talk below. I have 3 hours, a budget of 100, and want a social activity. Use only the supplied source. Make a table of verified facts, my preferences, unknowns, and claims I must check. Then compare the two. Do not choose for me, do not invent availability, and label uncertainty.

08 · The four labelsMinutes 45–52

Four labels, not one list

Fact

Stated in the source. 10:00–12:00, costs 45, book by Thursday.

Preference

Yours, and the page cannot know it. “I want something social.”

Assumption

Nobody said it. “There will probably be space without booking.”

Unknown

Still open. Whether a friend is free. How long the journey takes.

A tidy summary puts all four on the same line. The whole job is pulling them apart.

09 · VerificationMinutes 52–60

The tidy table is a draft, not a source

Check two claims. The two that would change the decision if they turned out to be wrong.

We go back to the original page because a decision changes when a fact changes. A confident answer is not evidence.

10 · When it goes wrongMinutes 60–78

Three failures, and what to say

It invented a detail
“Where did that come from?” — then move it to unknown
It chose for us
Back to “do not choose for me”, and run it again
It only shortened the page
“Which facts affect a decision, and what still needs checking?”

11 · Your judgmentMinutes 60–78

What it cannot decide for you

  1. What actually matters to you.
  2. How much uncertainty you will absorb.
  3. Whether to act now or find out more.

It organises. You decide. That is the division of labour for the whole course.
