2. Image prompt anatomy

A prompt that reliably produces a usable image tends to specify five things. Leave one out and the model guesses, and its guess is rarely the one you wanted.

  • Subject — what is actually in the frame
  • Composition — the angle and distance it's seen from
  • Light — the light source, direction, and time of day
  • Framing — where the empty space sits, for text or a logo later
  • Exclusions — what must not appear

Weak

“A nice photo of a garden.”

Strong

“A community garden bed in full bloom, seen at eye level from the path. Golden late-afternoon light from the left casting long shadows. Leave the upper-left quarter empty for a title. No people, no signage, no litter.”

Same intent, five specifics added. Read the strong prompt back against the list above — each clause answers one part.

3. Narration script pattern

A script that reads well on the page can still sound wrong out loud. Write for the ear:

  • Sentences — short. If a sentence needs a comma to survive, split it
  • One idea — per breath. One clause per sentence, not three joined with “and”
  • Numbers — spoken, not written: “about twelve thousand people,” not “12,847 attendees”
  • Names — pronunciation usually needs a second pass. Generate the narration, listen to it once, then fix names before recording again

4. The two traps

Both of these look free right up until they fail mid-session, with no recovery available on the spot.

Runway's credits are lifetime, not monthly

125 credits, once, ever — not a monthly allowance. It works for the first learner who tries it, then the account is empty for everyone after, with no reset before next session.

Kling AI watermarks every free export

There is no workaround on the free tier. It puts a visible mark on the exact artifact the session exists to produce — do not route a learner's final deliverable through it.
