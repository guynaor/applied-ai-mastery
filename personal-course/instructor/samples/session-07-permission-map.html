Sample app boundary — the capstone

A worked example of deliverable 5's boundary: what the app may touch, what always needs the learner, and what makes it stop. Use it as the shape, not as an answer — the boundary belongs to the app they actually built.

The app below is invented, and deliberately small. A boundary is easier to argue about when the app is simple enough to hold in your head.

The app

A packing list for the weekend trip in the capstone brief. It reads one file, adds to it, and ticks things off. That is all it does.

trip/
├── packing-list.txt     <- the app reads and writes this
├── budget.csv           <- the app has no reason to open this
└── photos/              <- nor this

The boundary

Three columns, and the third is the one people forget.

What it may touchWhat always needs meWhat makes it stop
packing-list.txt, and nothing else in the folder Adding an item that costs money, or anything that leaves the device — a message, an order, an upload A file it cannot find, a list longer than it expects, or a second run within the hour

Why the third column exists. The first two describe what the app should do. The third describes what happens when the world is not what the app assumed. An app with no stop condition does not fail — it keeps going, quietly, on wrong input.

Least privilege, in one sentence

The app can reach one file because it needs one file. It cannot reach budget.csv — not because that file is secret, but because the app has no reason to open it, and a reason is what access should require.

The test to apply to a learner's boundary: for each thing the app may touch, can they say what it needs it for? If not, that access does not belong in the list.

What a weak boundary looks like

What they wroteWhy it is not a boundary
"It only touches my own files"Which files? A folder is not a boundary — it is a location.
"I'll check what it does"An intention, not a rule. What specifically waits for you?
"It stops if there's an error"That is what crashing means. A stop condition is a case you chose to halt on while the app is still working.

What to say when reviewing one

The app is not finished without this table, and that is a "coming back", not a note in passing. The useful question is almost always the third column: "What would this do if the file were empty — and is that what you want?"
