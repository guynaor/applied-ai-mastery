# Session 6 — Tell a True Visual Story

**Time:** 45–60 minutes  
**Outcome:** A short visual story you are willing to stand behind — every claim supported, every image labelled correctly, and everyone in it consenting.

## Scenario

Pick something that actually happened and that you want to share: a trip, a project at home, a neighbourhood event, a personal milestone. The story should be interesting — without a generated or edited image doing the work of a record of something that did not happen. Do not post an image of another person without permission, and children always with a parent's approval.

## Sequence

1. **Set the trust boundary (8 minutes).** Write down who you are telling, what for, which claims you can support, and who agreed to appear. Claims you cannot support are not banned — they are simply worded differently.
2. **Sort the assets (10 minutes).** For each image or clip write: **original**, **edited**, or **generated** — and beside it where it came from and when, and what consent it carries. Keep a source next to every factual claim.
3. **Build one story (15 minutes).** Plan a six-image carousel, a short clip, or a before-and-after sequence. Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini can draft captions and alt text from your notes — they have not seen the place, so do not let them invent details.
4. **Check it through a viewer's eyes (12 minutes).** Add captions and alt text, check contrast, and clearly mark anything edited or generated. Then ask the only question that matters: **could a reasonable viewer take something untrue from this?** Fix it before you share.

## Integrated artifact

Create one story package: the storyboard or finished story, a record of sources and consents, captions and alt text, and a visible label on every edited or generated asset. The most common deception is not a fake image — it is a real image with a wrong caption.
