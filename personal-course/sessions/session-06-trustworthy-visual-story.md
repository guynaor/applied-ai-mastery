# Session 6 — From Prompt to Presentation

**Time:** 90 minutes  
**Outcome:** A short generated visual story — one image, two clips, a narration, and a deck built two ways — with every generated piece labelled and nothing mistaken for a photograph.

## Before this session

Create two accounts before the day, not during it: Google (stages 1 through 5) and Claude (stage 6) — both free, and together they complete the whole chain. That's one account fewer than this session used to need. If you already use ChatGPT, bring it too: stage 1 uses it for an optional comparison, but a Google account alone is a complete route on its own.

## Scenario

Pick one subject to carry through the whole chain: a place, a creature, an object, a small scene — real, invented, or a mix of both. Nothing in this session starts from a photograph. Stage 1 generates the first image from words alone; stage 2 repairs it; stage 3 animates the image you built; stage 4 generates a second clip from words alone, with nothing of yours in it. Three rules hold across all six stages: label anything generated, in the caption itself, not buried in a settings screen; never use a real person's likeness without consent, and a child's only with a parent's; and never let a generated piece stand in for a record of something that actually happened.

## Sequence

1. **Generate an image (11 minutes).** Write a prompt that names the subject, the composition, the light, the framing, and what to leave out. Run it in Gemini — that alone completes this stage in full. If you also use ChatGPT, run the same prompt there too and compare the two results: that comparison is the stage's real teaching point, and worth doing. A Gemini-only route misses nothing you will be assessed on.
2. **Fix it (11 minutes).** Do not throw the prompt away and re-roll: edit the region that is wrong, in the same tool you used for stage 1. Re-rolling destroys whatever already worked. Label the result before you move on — it carries that label for the rest of the chain.
3. **Animate your own image (8 minutes).** Upload the image you built and fixed into Gemini, and add a short prompt that steers the motion. The subject stays yours; the prompt only tells it how to move.
4. **Generate a clip from words alone (10 minutes).** Same tool, a text prompt with nothing else behind it — nothing of yours survives into this clip. Watch for where it breaks: text, hands, continuity, physics. Then ask yourself: would you publish either of today's two clips as a record of a real place? Neither is — so no. A label doesn't make it true; it stops it from deceiving. That's rule three, applied to something you made ten minutes ago.
5. **Narrate (9 minutes).** Write a short script that narrates the piece you've built — your subject, the image, and the two clips. For the ear, not the eye: short sentences, one idea each, numbers spoken rather than written. Record it in Google Cloud's speech studio: [https://console.cloud.google.com/agent-platform/studio/media/speech](https://console.cloud.google.com/agent-platform/studio/media/speech) — it is in the Cloud console, not aistudio.google.com. A second take usually fixes a mispronounced name faster than fighting with the first one.
6. **Assemble a deck, two routes (20 minutes).** Build the same short deck twice with Claude: once as a PPTX file you can open and edit in PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides, and once as a single self-contained HTML file that opens in any browser with nothing installed. The comparison is the point — notice what each format cost you and what it gave back.

## Integrated artifact

One production set: the generated image, the fixed version, the animated clip, the text-generated clip, the narration, and the deck — built by both routes — with a visible label on every piece that was generated. A convincing clip is not a record of something real.
