# Session 3 — Make a Shared Plan Work

**Time:** 45–60 minutes  
**Outcome:** A plan someone else can pick up and run without calling you, with a named owner on every task, a buffer, and one fallback.

## Scenario

Pick something you are genuinely organising with other people — a family event, a trip with friends, a house move, or a rota of household chores. Everyone involved has different availability, a different budget, different needs, and different preferences. The goal today is not an itinerary that looks good, but a plan people will actually work from. Keep other people's contact details, booking confirmations, and exact addresses out of an AI chat.

## Sequence

1. **Separate what is fixed from what is wanted (10 minutes).** List the dates that cannot move, the budget, the access needs, and the existing commitments — and beside them, separately, the preferences. Write one name: who decides when preferences collide. Most plans break right here, because a preference treated as a constraint locks everything else.
2. **Collect the facts that change (10 minutes).** Opening hours, travel times, prices, cancellation terms, and booking rules — each with its source and the date you checked it. These are exactly the details that were true last year and are not true now.
3. **Build one shareable plan (15 minutes).** Ask Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini to arrange **only the facts you supplied**: what is fixed first, then tasks, travel, breaks, and buffers. Every row needs an owner's name and a date. If an invitation or a poll is needed, word it so nobody has to hand over more than is necessary.
4. **Find where the plan breaks (10 minutes).** Take one realistic disruption — someone arrives late, it rains, the venue is full, or a key person does not reply — and run the plan against it. Add a fallback, a name for who triggers it, and a short list of what must be rechecked before anything is booked or sent.

## Integrated artifact

Create one shareable plan: the goal and constraints, who decides, a task table with owners and dates, the facts you checked with source and date, buffers, one fallback, and a recheck list. The people involved make the choices — not the tool and not the schedule.
