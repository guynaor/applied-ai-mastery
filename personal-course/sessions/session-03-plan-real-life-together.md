# Session 3 — Make a Shared Plan Work

**Time:** 90 minutes  
**Outcome:** A real event moved forward today — food, drinks, a running image display, a schedule, and divided work — with every tool output checked before you relied on it.

**How the session runs.** Your facilitator works through all of this live on screen while you watch, answer questions and argue with the results. You do not need a tool open, or an account, during the session itself. The sequence below is yours to run in the week afterwards, and the learning journal has every table waiting for it.


## Scenario

Pick something you are genuinely organising with other people: a family event, a party, a shared trip, a class evening. Today we are not learning a planning method — we are doing the work itself with AI, and after each job asking the same question: what did the tool assume, what did it invent, and who actually owns it. Keep contact details, exact addresses, and real guest lists out of the chat — roles and first names are enough.

## Sequence

1. **Food and drinks (10 minutes).** Give the tool your guest count, dietary constraints, and budget, and ask for quantities and a shopping list. Then the question that matters: **what did it assume about you?** Portions per person, how much people drink, whether there are children, how long the event runs — it will settle all of these with total confidence. Ask it to separate "what you told me" from "what I assumed".
2. **A running image display (13 minutes).** In **Claude Artifacts**, ask for one page that cycles images with captions, a pause control, and a speed you set. Use placeholder images for now. Two checks before you trust it: what happens with only two images, and what happens with a very long caption. And the question that arrives on its own: **whose photos are these, and who agreed to appear in them.**
3. **Timing (10 minutes).** Ask for a run sheet: when people arrive, when food lands, when the music starts, when you clear down. Now check **which of those facts change** — the venue's hours, when the caterer arrives and clears, when the last bus goes. Each one gets a source and a date, or it is an assumption.
4. **Dividing the work (12 minutes).** Ask for tasks split with owners and dates. The tool will write "someone should" — that is the line you fix. **Every task gets a name and a date.** And the thing the tool cannot know: who is genuinely free, and who has already taken on too much.
5. **What breaks (10 minutes).** Your facilitator posts the disruption cards in the chat during the session — take one. What breaks first? Add a fallback with three things: what happens instead, who triggers it, and by when it must be decided.

## Integrated artifact

Create one page you could send to whoever is helping you: food and drink quantities with the assumptions marked, the link to the image display, the run sheet with a source for every fact that changes, a task table with names and dates, and one fallback. If nobody but you can follow the page, it is not ready.
