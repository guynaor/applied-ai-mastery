# Session 3 — Make a Shared Plan Work

**Time:** 90 minutes  
**Outcome:** One real event — picked live, from one of you — becomes three files you could hand to somebody else: a planning sheet, a document with the run sheet and instructions, and a page that plays music and shows photos on the night.

**How the session works.** The facilitator does all of this live on screen while you watch, ask questions and argue with the results. You do not need a tool open, or an account, during the session itself. The sequence below is yours to run in the week that follows, and every table is waiting in the learning journal.

## Scenario

At the start of the session you will be asked what you are organising right now. **One of the answers gets picked, and we work on it for the whole session** — a real event, belonging to somebody in the room who can say "that is not true for me" when the tool gets it wrong.

We are not learning a planning method today — we are doing the work itself with AI, and after every file we ask the same question: what did the tool assume, what did it invent, and who actually owns it.

**When you suggest an event:** roles instead of surnames, no exact addresses, no phone numbers, no guest list. "The bride's mother" works fine. This is the same "what am I not willing to give a tool" lesson, except this time it happens live.

## Sequence

1. **The planning sheet — Excel (22 minutes).** Give the tool your guest count, constraints and budget, and ask for a file with one tab for quantities and a shopping list, and one tab for tasks with an owner and a date. The request that matters: **an "assumption" column it is not allowed to leave empty** — in every row, what it decided by itself without asking you. Portions per person, how much people drink an hour, whether children eat like adults. And on the task tab, the tool will write "somebody needs to send invitations" — **that is the line you fix into a name and a date.**
2. **The handover document — Word (18 minutes).** Ask for a file you can send to whoever runs the evening: an hour-by-hour run sheet, short operating instructions for each step, and a table of every fact that could change — the venue's hours, when catering arrives and leaves, the last bus — each with **a source and a date, or "assumption".** The only test that matters: if you go down with flu two days before, can somebody else run the evening from this file?
3. **The music and photos page — HTML (20 minutes).** Ask for one page that loops photos with captions and plays background music, running four hours untouched. It will almost certainly fail the first time **in two ways**, and that is the interesting part: the images do not appear because it pointed at internet addresses instead of files on your machine, and the music does not play because **every browser blocks audio until somebody clicks** — and a screen at an event never clicks. Two tests before you trust it: what happens with only two images, and with a very long caption. And the question that arrives by itself: **whose photos are these, who agreed to appear in them, and whose music is it.**
4. **What goes wrong (10 minutes).** The facilitator sends the disruption cards to the chat during the session — take one. What breaks first? Add a fallback with three things: what you do instead, who triggers it, and by when you have to decide.

## Integrated artifact

The three files travel together, and the test is whether they survive leaving your hands. The sheet carries the numbers with its assumptions still visible. The document carries the evening in an order somebody else can follow, with a source and a date on every fact that could move. The page carries the part that has to run on the night, off files that live next to it. Send all three to whoever is helping you — and if they come back with questions you could have answered in the files, they are not finished.

## Homework

**Build one of the three files for your own event** — not three, one. It can be the event picked in the session, or something else entirely that you are organising. The next session opens by asking what did not work, so notice where you got stuck.
