# Lesson 12 — Build Your Personal Brand

**Time:** 25–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Turn genuine experience and course projects into a truthful CV, LinkedIn summary, and small portfolio without exaggeration.

## The core idea

AI can help organize, tailor, and improve language. It must not invent experience, achievements, responsibilities, dates, tools, or results.

Your strongest material may come from the projects completed in this course:

- a researched purchase comparison
- a travel or event plan
- a mini app
- a CAD concept
- a visual-story project
- a structured decision or investment-research brief.

## Step 1 — Build an evidence bank

List 3–5 experiences or projects. For each record:

- what you actually did
- tools used
- decisions made
- problems solved
- evidence or output available
- result, only if known
- what you learned.

Avoid unsupported numbers such as “improved efficiency by 40%” unless you measured them.

## Step 2 — Choose a target

Select one real or sample opportunity:

- internship
- graduate role
- part-time job
- volunteer position
- university program
- freelance project.

Extract 5–8 important requirements from the description. Separate:

- required skills
- preferred skills
- responsibilities
- evidence you already have
- gaps you should not hide.

## Step 3 — Ask AI to map evidence, not invent it

Use:

> Compare this opportunity with my evidence bank. Create a table with: requirement, evidence I genuinely have, evidence gap, and truthful wording I could use. Do not invent experience or imply expertise beyond the evidence. Ask me questions where information is missing.

Review every suggested claim against your evidence bank.

## Step 4 — Draft one project entry

Use this structure:

**Project title — role or context**

- action taken
- method or tool
- problem or constraint
- result or artifact
- verification or limitation.

Example:

> Built a single-file browser poll using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and local storage; documented that votes remain device-local and tested label editing, reset behavior, and accessibility.

## Step 5 — Draft a short profile

Write 3–4 lines that explain:

- current stage
- areas of interest
- demonstrated skills
- kind of opportunity sought.

Do not use vague claims such as “world-class,” “expert,” or “highly experienced” without evidence.

## Step 6 — Build a small portfolio index

Choose 2–4 projects. For each include:

- title
- one-sentence problem
- your contribution
- artifact or screenshot
- tools
- what you verified
- what remains limited.

Remove private information, API keys, personal addresses, private photos, and other people’s data.

## Step 7 — Tailor and track

Create an application record with:

- organization and role
- source link
- date found
- deadline
- CV version used
- message or cover letter version
- status
- follow-up date
- response and next action.

## Completion check

You are done when you have:

- an evidence bank
- one target-role analysis
- one truthful project entry
- one short profile
- a 2–4 project portfolio index
- an application-tracking record.

Reusable rule: **AI may improve the presentation of your evidence; it may not create the evidence.**
