# Instructor Script — Lesson 2: Summarize Without Losing What Matters

**Duration:** 25 minutes  
**Outcome:** The student creates a purpose-specific summary, verifies important claims, and converts supported information into organized notes, tasks, and reminders.

## Definitions

- **Purpose-specific summary:** a summary designed for a particular use rather than generic shortening.
- **Traceability:** the ability to connect a summary statement to the source.
- **Extraction:** copying or restructuring information that is explicitly present.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from the source but not directly stated.
- **Unsupported addition:** information that does not appear in the source and is not clearly labelled as inference.
- **Action item:** a defined task, preferably with owner and deadline.

## Preparation

1. Open the student activity and verification worksheet.
2. Prepare a short public source containing facts, a date, an action, and one missing detail.
3. Ensure the student has a safe source with no sensitive personal content.
4. Prepare a notes or task app, or use a plain document if unavailable.

## 0–3 minutes — Hook

Show a generic summary of the source and ask:

> “Shorter for what purpose?”

Explain that the same source needs different summaries for studying, acting, deciding, or briefing someone.

## 3–7 minutes — Define the requested output

Ask the student to finish: “I need this summary so I can…”

Reject vague answers such as “understand it better.” Ask what action or decision follows.

Introduce the five sections: key points, decisions, actions, unresolved questions, and verification items.

## 7–13 minutes — Generate the structured summary

The student runs the supplied prompt using their source.

Review the output together. Ask:

- “Which section is directly extracted?”
- “Where did the model infer something?”
- “Did it invent an owner or deadline?”
- “What important uncertainty remains?”

Gate: missing information must be labelled, not filled with guesses.

## 13–18 minutes — Verify three claims

Have the student choose the three most consequential statements, not the easiest ones.

For each, require the exact supporting passage or a correction.

Say:

> “A polished sentence is not evidence. Find where the source supports it.”

If a statement is partly supported, split it into supported and unsupported parts.

## 18–22 minutes — Convert to digital-life outputs

The student creates tasks, calendar items, and follow-up questions only from supported information.

Check:

- tasks have clear verbs
- owners and deadlines are not invented
- reminders include enough context
- the note title is searchable
- follow-ups include who or what can resolve them.

## 22–24 minutes — Compare formats

Have the student request one alternate format. Ask which format better matches the stated purpose and why.

## 24–25 minutes — Exit check

Ask:

1. Why can a summary be accurate but still unhelpful?
2. What is traceability?
3. What should happen when a deadline is not stated?
4. What is the difference between extraction and inference?

## Common mistakes and interventions

- **Student accepts every sentence:** Require source evidence for three consequential claims.
- **AI invents deadlines:** Replace them with `not stated` and add a follow-up question.
- **Summary is too broad:** Return to the purpose statement.
- **Tasks have no owner:** Mark owner unknown rather than guessing.
- **Student uploads sensitive messages:** Stop and use a public or anonymized source.

## Minimum evidence

The student submits a purpose statement, structured summary, three verification rows, corrected unsupported content, and at least one organized digital-life output supported by the source.
