# Lesson 2 — Summarize Without Losing What Matters

**Time:** 20–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Turn a long piece of information into a reliable summary, action list, follow-up questions, and calendar-ready items.

## The core idea

A useful summary is not simply shorter text. It should preserve the information needed for its purpose.

Before asking AI to summarize, decide which output you need:

- overview
- study notes
- decisions made
- tasks and owners
- deadlines and dates
- unresolved questions
- information that needs verification.

## Choose your source

Use one non-sensitive item that is long enough to contain several ideas:

- a public article
- lecture notes
- a club or group announcement
- a long email you wrote yourself
- meeting notes with names removed
- a public video transcript.

Do not upload private messages or copyrighted material you are not allowed to share.

## Step 1 — Define the purpose

Complete:

> I need this summary so I can **[purpose]**.

Examples: prepare for a quiz, understand a decision, know what to do next, or brief a friend.

## Step 2 — Ask for a structured summary

Use this prompt:

> Summarize the material for this purpose: **[purpose]**. Separate the result into:
> 1. key points
> 2. decisions or conclusions
> 3. actions, owners, and deadlines
> 4. unresolved questions
> 5. claims or dates that should be checked against the source.
> Do not add facts that are not in the material. Use “not stated” where information is missing.

Select three important statements from the summary. For each, find the supporting passage in the original.

## Step 4 — Turn information into a digital-life system

Create these outputs only when supported:

- **Tasks:** action, owner, due date, status
- **Calendar items:** title, date/time, preparation reminder
- **Follow-up list:** question, person/source to ask, review date
- **Notes title:** a searchable name with topic and date.

Do not invent deadlines. Mark missing owners or dates as `not stated`.

## Step 5 — Compare two summary styles

Ask the AI for one alternative:

- a five-bullet executive summary
- study flashcards
- a timeline
- a decision-and-action table.

Write which format is more useful for your purpose and why.

## Completion check

You are done when you have:

- a purpose statement
- a structured summary
- three source checks
- corrected unsupported content
- tasks, calendar items, or follow-ups only where justified
- a chosen summary format and reason.

Reusable rule: **A trustworthy summary can be traced back to its source.**
