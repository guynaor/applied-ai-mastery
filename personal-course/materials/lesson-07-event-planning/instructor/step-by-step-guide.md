# Step-by-Step Instructor Guide — Lesson 7

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Teach collaborative planning without letting AI invent consensus or hide responsibility.

## Definitions

- **Hard constraint:** A requirement the plan must satisfy.
- **Preference:** A desirable feature that may be traded off.
- **Owner:** The one person accountable for completing a task.
- **Dependency:** Something that must happen before another task can proceed.
- **Fallback:** A prepared alternative used when a disruption occurs.
- **Confirmed fact:** Information verified by the responsible person or source.

## Preparation

Open the student activity and workbook. Have a simple example ready, such as a six-person picnic with a €120 ceiling. Do not prepare a finished answer.

## 0–3 minutes — Hook

Say:

> “Most group plans fail because everyone discusses ideas, but nobody owns the next action.”

Ask the student for one event they may actually organize. Keep the event small enough for the lesson.

## 3–7 minutes — Define the boundary

Have the student complete the event brief and separate constraints from preferences.

Ask:

- “What cannot be violated?”
- “What is only desirable?”
- “Which fact must be checked rather than guessed?”

Correct any budget, date, accessibility, or transport need incorrectly treated as optional.

## 7–11 minutes — Build the poll

The student asks AI for no more than five questions. Review each question together.

Reject questions that:

- do not affect a decision;
- collect unnecessary personal information;
- combine two decisions into one question;
- offer choices that violate a hard constraint.

Checkpoint: the poll must produce information that can change the plan.

## 11–15 minutes — Interpret responses

Use real or fictional responses. Ask the student to distinguish:

- clear agreement;
- disagreement;
- missing response;
- a decision that still requires human judgment.

Say:

> “AI may summarize the responses, but it cannot claim agreement that is not present.”

## 15–20 minutes — Create ownership

Build the action board. Require one owner and one deadline per task.

Ask “Who will notice if this does not happen?” If the answer is unclear, ownership is unclear.

Add reminder dates to a calendar or task system. Explain that digital organization is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## 20–24 minutes — Invitation and poster prompt

The student drafts an invitation using confirmed facts only. Check that pending details are labelled. Review the poster prompt for privacy, consent, and truthful representation.

## 24–28 minutes — Stress test

Introduce one disruption. Before editing, ask what is directly affected and what remains unchanged. Require updates to the action board and invitation only where necessary.

## Exit check

The student explains the difference between a preference and a constraint, identifies the owner of the most important task, and names one unconfirmed fact.

## Minimum acceptable output

- event brief;
- short decision poll;
- response summary without invented consensus;
- action board with owners, deadlines, and reminders;
- truthful invitation;
- tested fallback.

## Interventions

- **“Everyone” owns a task:** assign one accountable person.
- **AI invents agreement:** return to the actual responses.
- **Invitation includes a guess:** label it pending or remove it.
- **Plan is too large:** reduce the guest count or decisions.
- **Student focuses on poster styling:** finish decisions and ownership first.
