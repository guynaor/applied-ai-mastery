# Lesson 4 — Buy Smarter Online

**Time:** 20–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Compare products using real needs, total cost, seller reliability, return terms, and evidence—not marketing language alone.

## The core idea

AI can organize product research, expose missing questions, and compare trade-offs. It cannot guarantee that a listing is accurate, a review is genuine, or a current price will remain available.

## Choose a purchase

Pick a real or realistic purchase costing enough that comparison matters. Good examples include headphones, a laptop, an office chair, luggage, a small appliance, or sports equipment.

Use no more than **three candidate products**.

## Step 1 — Define the need before searching

Write:

- intended use;
- maximum budget;
- three must-have requirements;
- two nice-to-have features;
- deal-breakers;
- how long you expect to keep the product.

## Step 2 — Build a comparison record

For each candidate, record:

- exact model;
- seller;
- listed price;
- delivery cost;
- taxes or import charges if relevant;
- required accessories;
- warranty;
- return window and return cost;
- evidence source and date checked.

Calculate **total expected purchase cost**, not only the advertised price.

## Step 3 — Ask AI to challenge the comparison

Use:

> I am comparing these products for this use: **[use]**. My must-haves are **[requirements]** and my deal-breakers are **[deal-breakers]**. Review my comparison and identify missing costs, compatibility questions, seller risks, misleading claims, and facts that require verification. Do not invent specifications or current prices.

Add only relevant questions to your worksheet.

## Step 4 — Inspect evidence quality

For every important claim, label the source:

- manufacturer specification;
- retailer listing;
- independent review;
- owner review;
- assumption or unknown.

Do not treat repeated retailer text as independent evidence.

## Step 5 — Make a bounded recommendation

Choose one:

- buy now;
- wait for verification or a price threshold;
- reject all current options.

Record:

- top reason;
- strongest downside;
- one fact to verify before paying;
- return deadline to save after purchase;
- receipt, warranty, and serial-number storage plan.

## Completion check

You are done when you have:

- a need statement created before product comparison;
- no more than three candidates;
- total-cost comparison;
- source labels and verification dates;
- seller, warranty, and return checks;
- a bounded recommendation.

Reusable rule: **Compare the complete ownership decision, not the biggest number on the product page.**
