# Instructor Script — Lesson 4: Buy Smarter Online

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes

## Definitions

- **Total expected cost:** listed price plus delivery, taxes, required accessories, and foreseeable fees.
- **Source type:** who produced a claim and for what purpose.
- **Independent evidence:** evidence not copied from the same original claim.
- **Compatibility:** whether the product works with the student's existing devices, space, standards, or use.
- **Bounded recommendation:** buy, wait, or reject, with limits and verification conditions.

## Preparation

Open the activity and workbook. Ask the student to choose one realistic purchase and at most three candidates. Do not let the lesson turn into unrestricted web browsing.

## 0–4 minutes — Start with the need

Say: “A good comparison starts before the product pages. Otherwise marketing decides the criteria for us.”

Require the student to complete use, budget, must-haves, nice-to-haves, deal-breakers, and expected lifetime.

Checkpoint: reject vague requirements such as “good quality.” Ask what observable feature would demonstrate it.

## 4–12 minutes — Build the full-cost comparison

Have the student fill the candidate table. For each price ask:

- Is delivery included?
- Are accessories required?
- Could tax or import fees apply?
- Is the seller the manufacturer, an authorized retailer, or a marketplace seller?
- What happens if it must be returned?

Explain that an unavailable cost stays marked unknown; it is not silently treated as zero.

## 12–18 minutes — Challenge with AI

Run the supplied challenge prompt. Ask the student to accept only useful questions, not every suggestion.

Expected useful categories: compatibility, seller identity, warranty region, consumables, return shipping, exaggerated claims, and missing comparison criteria.

## 18–23 minutes — Label evidence

Choose three consequential claims and label their source type. Ask whether two pages repeat the same manufacturer statement.

Intervene if the student treats owner ratings as proof of a technical specification or assumes many repeated claims are independent.

## 23–28 minutes — Decide and organize follow-up

Require buy, wait, or reject. The decision must include a downside and one pre-payment verification.

If buying, have the student plan where the receipt, warranty, serial number, and return deadline will be stored. If waiting, record a price threshold and review date.

## Exit check

The student explains the difference between advertised price and total expected cost, and identifies one claim that still requires verification.
