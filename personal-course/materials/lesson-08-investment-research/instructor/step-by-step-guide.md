# Step-by-Step Instructor Guide — Lesson 8

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Teach evidence-balanced financial research without predictions, recommendations, or false certainty.

## Definitions

- **Primary source:** Original information such as a filing, official factsheet, or regulator record.
- **Independent evidence:** Evidence not merely repeating the same original claim.
- **Concentration risk:** Dependence on a small number of holdings, sectors, regions, or factors.
- **Liquidity:** How easily an asset can be bought or sold without major delay or price impact.
- **Stale information:** Information too old to support the current question.
- **Suitability:** Whether an investment fits a particular person’s goals, finances, time horizon, and risk tolerance. This lesson does not determine suitability.

## Preparation

Open the activity and workbook. Prepare a neutral example such as comparing what a broad-market ETF holds, not whether it should be purchased.

## 0–4 minutes — Boundary and question

Say:

> “AI can help us read and organize financial information. It cannot reliably predict returns, and this lesson does not make a personal investment recommendation.”

Have the student rewrite any predictive question into a factual research question.

## 4–9 minutes — Source quality

Ask the student to classify three sources by type and identify whether one repeats another.

Questions:

- “Who produced this information?”
- “What incentive might they have?”
- “Is this original evidence or commentary?”
- “When could it become stale?”

Checkpoint: at least one primary source and one independent perspective are visible.

## 9–15 minutes — Balanced brief

The student runs the supplied prompt using only provided information. Require citations to the source register.

Stop the student if the output includes:

- a price target
- a confident forecast
- personalized allocation advice
- unsupported claims about safety
- omitted fees or risks.

## 15–20 minutes — Challenge weak claims

Run the weak-claim prompt. Select one claim and trace it back to the original source.

Ask whether the source supports the exact wording or only a weaker statement. Revise the claim accordingly.

## 20–25 minutes — Risks, unknowns, and review trigger

Require risks from more than one category and at least one item the student does not understand. Add a review trigger tied to a future event or source update.

## 25–28 minutes — Reflection

Ask:

1. “What did AI make easier?”
2. “What could it not verify?”
3. “Why is balanced evidence not the same as suitability?”

## Minimum acceptable output

- non-predictive question
- source register with dependence noted
- balanced brief with citations
- three weak claims reviewed
- risks and unknowns
- review trigger.

## Interventions

- **Student asks what to buy:** return to the research question.
- **Three articles repeat one claim:** count the original evidence once.
- **AI predicts performance:** remove the prediction and document the missing evidence.
- **Only positive evidence appears:** require the strongest opposing case.
- **Student enters private financial details:** replace them with a fictional or generalized scenario.
