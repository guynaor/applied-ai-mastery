# Lesson 8 — Research Investing Responsibly

**Time:** 20–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Use AI to organize balanced investment research without treating it as a prediction engine or personalized financial adviser.

## Important boundary

This lesson teaches research discipline. It does not tell you what to buy, how much to invest, or whether an investment is suitable for you.

## Step 1 — Choose one research question

Examples:

- What does this ETF actually hold?
- What are the main risks of this company?
- How does a bond fund differ from a stock fund?
- What fees and concentration risks matter?

Avoid questions such as “Will this stock go up?”

## Step 2 — Define the research frame

Write:

- the asset or category;
- the purpose of the research;
- the date of the information;
- what decision this research will **not** make for you.

## Step 3 — Gather at least three source types

Use a mix such as:

- official fund or company material;
- regulator or exchange information;
- audited filings or reports;
- independent analysis or reputable financial journalism.

Do not count several pages repeating the same original claim as independent evidence.

## Step 4 — Ask AI for a balanced brief

Use:

> Using only the information I provide, create a balanced research brief with: what the asset is, how it may generate returns, major risks, fees or costs, concentration, liquidity, evidence supporting the case, evidence against the case, missing information, and facts that may become stale. Do not predict returns or recommend a purchase. Cite each claim to the supplied source.

## Step 5 — Challenge the output

Ask AI:

> Identify the three weakest claims in this brief. Explain whether each is unsupported, stale, dependent on one source, or based on an assumption.

Check those claims against the original sources.

## Step 6 — Build a risk and unknowns list

Include at least:

- market or price risk;
- concentration risk;
- fees and taxes not yet evaluated;
- liquidity or lock-up issues where relevant;
- currency risk where relevant;
- what you still do not understand.

## Step 7 — Set a review trigger

Record one condition that would make the research stale, such as a new annual report, fee change, major holding change, or personal financial change.

## Completion check

- [ ] The question is researchable, not predictive.
- [ ] At least three source types were used.
- [ ] Repeated claims were not treated as independent proof.
- [ ] Supporting and opposing evidence are both visible.
- [ ] Weak claims were checked against sources.
- [ ] A review trigger is recorded.

Reusable rule: **AI can structure evidence, but uncertainty and suitability remain human responsibilities.**
