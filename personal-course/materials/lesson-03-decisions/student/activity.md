# Lesson 3 — Make Better Decisions

**Time:** 20–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Compare real options using explicit criteria, evidence, assumptions, and a review date—without letting AI make the decision for you.

## The core idea

AI is useful for organizing a decision, finding missing questions, and testing how priorities affect the result. It should not hide value judgments inside a confident recommendation.

A good decision record separates:

- **facts:** supported information;
- **preferences:** what matters to you;
- **assumptions:** unverified beliefs used temporarily;
- **unknowns:** information still needed;
- **trade-offs:** what improves and worsens with each option.

## Choose a decision

Pick a real, reversible or low-risk decision with **2–3 options**. Examples:

- choose a course, laptop, phone plan, gym, or apartment viewing;
- decide between travel dates;
- choose how to spend a free weekend;
- select a study method;
- compare transport options.

Do not use this exercise as the sole basis for medical, legal, high-risk financial, or safety decisions.

## Step 1 — Define the decision

Complete:

> I need to choose **[one decision]** by **[date]**. The options are **[options]**.

Write what would make the decision successful six weeks or six months later.

## Step 2 — Choose criteria before scoring

Select 4–6 criteria that genuinely matter. Examples: cost, time, flexibility, quality, risk, convenience, learning value, social fit, or reversibility.

Give each criterion an importance weight from 1–5.

| Criterion | Why it matters | Weight 1–5 |
|---|---|---:|
| | | |

## Step 3 — Ask AI to challenge the frame

Use:

> I am comparing **[options]** using these criteria: **[criteria]**. Before recommending anything, identify:
> 1. missing criteria;
> 2. hidden assumptions;
> 3. information I should verify;
> 4. ways my framing could bias the result.
> Do not invent facts about the options.

Add only the suggestions that genuinely matter.

## Step 4 — Build the matrix

Score each option from 1–5 for each criterion. Every factual score needs a source, observation, or `assumption` label.

Weighted score = score × weight.

The total is a thinking aid, not an automatic answer.

## Step 5 — Run a sensitivity check

Change one important weight or uncertain score. Ask:

- Does the preferred option change?
- Which assumption has the greatest influence?
- Is the decision robust or fragile?

## Step 6 — Make and record the decision

Write:

- current choice;
- top two reasons;
- strongest downside;
- information still missing;
- what would cause reconsideration;
- review date.

Add the review date to your calendar if the decision can be revisited.

## Completion check

You are done when you have:

- a clear decision and options;
- criteria chosen before scoring;
- weights with reasons;
- fact/assumption labels;
- one sensitivity test;
- a decision record and review date.

Reusable rule: **Use AI to reveal the structure of a decision, not to outsource your values.**
