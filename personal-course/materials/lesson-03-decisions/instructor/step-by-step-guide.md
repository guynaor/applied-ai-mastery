# Instructor Script — Lesson 3: Make Better Decisions

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes  
**Outcome:** The student structures a real decision, separates facts from assumptions, tests sensitivity, and records when the decision should be reviewed.

## Definitions

- **Criterion:** a factor used to compare options.
- **Weight:** how important a criterion is to the decision-maker.
- **Score:** how well an option performs against a criterion.
- **Assumption:** an unverified belief temporarily used in the analysis.
- **Sensitivity check:** changing one important input to see whether the result changes.
- **Robust decision:** a choice that remains preferred under reasonable input changes.
- **Fragile decision:** a choice that changes when one uncertain input changes slightly.
- **Review date:** a planned time to revisit a decision when new evidence may exist.

## Preparation

1. Open the student activity and decision workbook.
2. Prepare a simple example with three options, such as transport methods or laptop choices.
3. Keep the example low-risk and reversible.
4. Have a calculator or spreadsheet available, though manual arithmetic is sufficient.

## 0–3 minutes — Hook

Ask:

> “Would you rather receive a confident recommendation or understand what makes the recommendation change?”

Explain that AI can organize the comparison, but the student owns the values and final choice.

## 3–7 minutes — Define the decision precisely

Have the student state one decision, deadline, options, and desired outcome.

Reject bundled questions such as “Where should I live and what career should I choose?” Narrow to one decision.

Ask:

- “Is this decision reversible?”
- “What does a good outcome look like later?”
- “What option is missing?”

## 7–11 minutes — Choose criteria before scoring

The student selects 4–6 criteria and explains each weight.

Say:

> “Choose the rules before seeing which option wins.”

Watch for duplicate criteria, such as `cheap` and `low price`, which double-count the same preference.

Gate: every criterion must be understandable and every weight must have a reason.

## 11–15 minutes — Use AI to challenge the framing

The student runs the challenge prompt.

Review suggestions together. Do not add every AI-generated criterion. Ask:

- “Would this criterion actually change your choice?”
- “Is this your value or the AI's assumption?”
- “What information would verify this?”

Require at least one hidden assumption or missing-information item to be recorded.

## 15–21 minutes — Score with provenance

The student scores each option. For each important score, require one of:

- source;
- direct observation;
- calculation;
- explicit `assumption` label.

Explain weighted scoring briefly. Do not allow the total to be treated as a command.

Ask which score is least certain and which criterion dominates the total.

## 21–25 minutes — Sensitivity check

Change exactly one influential weight or uncertain score.

Before recalculating, ask the student to predict whether the result will change.

If the preferred option changes, label the decision fragile and identify what evidence is needed before committing. If it does not, explain why that increases confidence but does not prove correctness.

## 25–28 minutes — Record the decision

The student records the current choice, reasons, downside, unknowns, reconsideration trigger, and review date.

Add the review date to a calendar or task list when relevant.

## 28–30 minutes — Exit check

Ask:

1. Why choose criteria before scoring?
2. What is the difference between a fact and an assumption?
3. What does a sensitivity check reveal?
4. Why might a decision need a review date?

## Common mistakes and interventions

- **Student asks AI to decide immediately:** Return to criteria and values.
- **Criteria overlap:** Merge them or explain the intended distinction.
- **Scores look precise but lack evidence:** Add provenance or label assumptions.
- **Student follows the highest total blindly:** Require downside and reconsideration trigger.
- **No review date:** Ask what future information could change the choice.
- **High-stakes decision:** Reframe as research support and identify the appropriate qualified human.

## Minimum evidence

The student submits a defined decision, weighted criteria, sourced or labelled scores, one sensitivity test, and a decision record with a reconsideration trigger and review date.
