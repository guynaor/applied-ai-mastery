# Instructor Script — Lesson 6: Plan a Realistic Trip

**Duration:** 25–30 minutes

## Definitions

- **Hard constraint:** a requirement the plan must not violate.
- **Preference:** desirable but negotiable.
- **Travel origin:** where a travel-time estimate begins.
- **Buffer:** reserved time for ordinary uncertainty.
- **Contingency:** a predefined response to a disruption.
- **Staged verification:** checking volatile facts at the time when the check is useful.

## 0–5 minutes — Frame the trip

Ask the student to define destination, dates, arrival/departure, budget, must-do activity, and any rest, access, or food constraints.

Say: “We first prove the plan is feasible. Only then do we add attractive extras.”

## 5–11 minutes — Classify constraints

Have the student mark each requirement hard or preferred. Ask what happens if it is missed.

Intervene if rest, accessibility, fixed bookings, or departure timing are treated as optional.

## 11–17 minutes — Build a small candidate set

Limit the plan to a few activities. For each, require location, window, duration, cost, booking status, travel origin/time, source, and check date.

Unknown opening hours or travel times remain flagged; they are not invented by AI.

## 17–21 minutes — Generate and inspect the itinerary

Run the supplied prompt. Review fixed commitments first, then travel, rest, buffers, and preferences.

Ask at every transition: “From where does this travel begin?” and “What happens if the prior activity runs late?”

## 21–25 minutes — Stress-test

Apply the 90-minute arrival delay and unavailable-activity cases. Require the student to preserve hard constraints and explain what is dropped or moved.

## 25–30 minutes — Build the digital handoff

Require calendar-ready events, shared notes, task owners, confirmations, and the three-stage verification checklist: before booking, 48 hours before, and same day.

Remind the student to keep passport, payment, and sensitive identity information outside general AI prompts.

## Exit check

The student identifies the most fragile part of the itinerary, its early warning sign, and the contingency that protects the plan.
